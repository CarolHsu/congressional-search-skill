# Selection and CTA Framework

## 1. Member classification (Taiwan / China record)

Research recent votes, sponsored and cosponsored Taiwan legislation, statements, Taiwan Caucus participation, Indo-Pacific/defense/trade/tech/supply-chain positions, and Taiwan visits. Classify, then justify in 2–3 sentences:

- **A — CHAMPION**: actively leads Taiwan legislation or advocacy.
- **B — SUPPORTER**: consistently supports, rarely leads.
- **C — PERSUADABLE**: limited record, interests suggest alignment.
- **D — SKEPTIC / CONSTRAINED**: likely concerns (security, spending, escalation, trade).
- **E — UNKNOWN**: insufficient meaningful record.

## 2. Scoring every issue

Evaluate EVERY in-scope issue before choosing. Do not jump straight to three bills.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Committee leverage | 30% |
| Policy-interest fit | 25% |
| Legislative timing / actionability | 20% |
| Existing Taiwan record | 15% |
| District / state relevance | 10% |

Mathematical precision is not required, but the ranking must visibly follow these criteria.

Ideal portfolio (guidance, not a rule; pick the strongest three overall):
1. HIGH-PROBABILITY ASK: naturally aligned with the member.
2. HIGH-LEVERAGE ASK: the member has real procedural or political influence.
3. STRATEGIC EXPANSION: could deepen the member's Taiwan engagement.

Always include WHY NOT THE OTHERS: one concise reason per unselected issue. Optimize for "what can we realistically get THIS member to do?", not "what is generally important to Taiwan."

## 3. Legislative status → CTA matrix

For each selected bill determine: status, committee jurisdiction, whether the member sits on it, whether already a sponsor/cosponsor, whether a substitute/amended/package vehicle exists, and what power this member has right now.

| Status | Member situation | CTA |
|---|---|---|
| Introduced / referred | Off committee, not cosponsor | PRIMARY: cosponsor |
| Introduced / referred | On committee, not cosponsor | Cosponsor + support hearing/markup |
| Introduced / referred | Already cosponsor | Thank + ask to help advance (e.g. press committee leadership) |
| Markup pending | On committee | Vote YES, support markup, oppose harmful amendments |
| Markup pending | Off committee | Cosponsor if appropriate + encourage committee/leadership action |
| Passed committee | Not cosponsor | Cosponsor may still be primary if bill is active; secondary: support floor consideration, vote YES |
| Passed committee | Cosponsor | Support floor consideration, vote YES, encourage leadership |
| Language in another vehicle | Standalone still meaningful, not cosponsor | PRIMARY: cosponsor standalone; SECONDARY: support/preserve provisions in active vehicle |
| Language in another vehicle | Standalone obsolete | Support/preserve language in active vehicle |
| Passed House (House member) | — | Do not spend a primary ask re-passing it; thank if they supported it; usually prioritize another issue |
| Passed Senate, not House (House member) | — | Support House companion / relevant House vehicle |
| Conference / NDAA / reauthorization | — | Preserve the provision in final legislation |
| Signed into law | — | No passage ask; implementation/oversight only if relevant |

Do NOT downgrade a possible cosponsorship into vague "support" just because another vehicle is further along. A cosponsor has made a public, trackable commitment; "support" is not trackable.

## 4. CTA hierarchy (per selected issue)

- **PRIMARY CTA**: strongest realistic, concrete, measurable commitment.
- **SECONDARY / FALLBACK**: what to ask if the primary is declined.
- **STRETCH CTA**: higher-leverage action if the member is especially receptive.
- **DO-NOT-ASK**: procedurally inappropriate, obsolete, redundant, or needlessly weak.

Prefer: cosponsor; vote YES; support markup; request a hearing; encourage committee or chamber leadership; sign or lead a congressional letter; preserve language in a vehicle; join the Congressional Taiwan Caucus; lead legislation.

Avoid "Please support Taiwan" and "Please support this bill" when a measurable action exists.

## 5. Why this member (framing)

For each selected issue, connect the legislation to something real: committee work, caucus leadership, professional background, district industry, prior votes, constituent community, or topic areas such as technology, manufacturing, labor, transportation, agriculture, defense, education, human rights, trade.

Label each connection VERIFIED CONNECTION (direct evidence) or STRATEGIC INFERENCE (useful framing, not evidence). Example of the right standard:

- BAD: "The Innovation Caucus supports TASA."
- GOOD: "Rep. X chairs the Innovation Caucus. TASA involves U.S.–Taiwan technology and space cooperation, so innovation is a useful thematic bridge. This is a strategic connection, not evidence that the caucus supports TASA."
